<Section label="The problem">

Most repairs happen out of sight: you hand over your phone, wait, pay and hope. Open Bench is a fictional mail-in repair workshop I created where you can watch: a fixed price first, a padded box in the post, and a photo of every step on the bench.

I designed the brand and the product in Figma, then built the site in Next.js with the brand tokens in Tailwind.

</Section>

<Section label="What I built">

- A landing page with a working quote: pick your device and what's wrong, and the fixed price appears straight away.
- An in-app quote flow where you can pick several faults at once. The fixed price adds them up, and anything without a listed price becomes a free check.
- Demo sign-up and log-in with a one-click demo account. Everything lives in the browser tab, so nothing you type is stored.
- A repairs list and a repair page with a timeline, a photo log of each step, a question when the technician finds something extra, and payment once it's fixed.
- Tests for the quote, repair and session logic using Node's built-in test runner.

</Section>

<Section label="Accessibility">

The audience includes parents, older people and people with low vision, so the bar was WCAG 2.2 AA from 320px phones up.

- A custom Select that follows the combobox pattern, keyboard-friendly tabs, a labelled progress bar and live regions that announce changes.
- I checked every page and state with axe and fixed what it found: one main landmark per page, the right heading order, and clear hover, focus and disabled states.

</Section>
